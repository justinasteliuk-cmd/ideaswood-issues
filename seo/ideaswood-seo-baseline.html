### H1: Log Cabin Wall Thickness: Which One Do You Need?
Wall thickness decides how warm, strong and quiet your cabin is – and how much it costs. The short answer:

| Wall | Best for | Year-round use | Typical size |
|---|---|---|---|
| **28mm** | Storage, summer house, kids' play room | No – seasonal | up to ~12 m² |
| **44mm** | Garden office, studio, gym, hobby room | Yes, with floor & roof insulation + heating | 10–30 m² |
| **70mm** | Guest house, holiday let, living space | Yes – the best choice for daily use | 20 m²+ |

#### 28mm – light and affordable
Enough for tools, bikes and summer use. Keeps rain out and stays stable, but gets cold in winter. Choose it when you need space, not comfort.

#### 44mm – the garden office standard
The most popular choice in the UK and Europe. Solid enough for a heated office or gym all year. Add floor and roof insulation and a small heater and it is comfortable in winter.

#### 70mm – for living in
Thick logs hold heat, block noise and carry bigger spans and heavier roofs. The right choice for guest houses, holiday rentals and residential cabins.

#### What else matters (as much as thickness)
- **Timber quality:** slow-grown Nordic spruce has tighter rings – less twisting and cracking.
- **Log profile:** double tongue-and-groove joints seal better than single.
- **Windows and doors:** double glazing in all our 44mm+ cabins. [verify]
- **Insulation:** floor and roof lose more heat than walls. See our insulation kit guide.
- **Foundation:** level base, ventilation under the floor.

#### FAQ
**Is 44mm thick enough to use all year?** Yes, for an office or studio, if you insulate the floor and roof and add heating.
**Do I need 70mm to live in a log cabin?** For daily living, yes. Most residential log homes use 70mm+ or insulated double walls.
**Does a thicker wall need a bigger foundation?** The base must carry more weight; ask us for loads per model.

## H1: Do You Need Planning Permission for a Log Cabin?
In England, most garden log cabins do **not** need planning permission. They count as "permitted development" (Class E outbuildings) if they meet these rules:

### Permitted development rules (England)
- **Not in front of the house:** not forward of the principal elevation facing a road.
- **Height:** single storey, eaves max **2.5 m**; total height max **4 m** with a dual-pitched roof or **3 m** for any other roof.
- **Near the boundary:** within **2 m** of a boundary, total height max **2.5 m**.
- **Coverage:** outbuildings and extensions must not cover more than **50%** of the land around the original house.
- **Use:** must be "incidental" to the house – office, gym, studio, hobby room, storage. Not a separate home or a bedroom.
- **No verandas, balconies or raised platforms** over 0.3 m high.

### When you DO need planning permission
- You want to sleep or live in it, or rent it out as a separate dwelling.
- Listed building, or land in a conservation area, National Park, AONB/National Landscape or World Heritage Site (stricter limits: e.g. outbuildings more than 20 m from the house limited to 10 m² in designated land).
- Flats, maisonettes, or where permitted development rights were removed.

### Building regulations (separate from planning)
- **Under 15 m²** with no sleeping accommodation: usually exempt.
- **15–30 m²**, no sleeping accommodation: exempt if at least **1 m from the boundary** or built from non-combustible materials.
- **Over 30 m²**, or with sleeping accommodation: building regulations apply.
- Electrics must meet Part P – use a registered electrician.

### Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland
Each nation has its own permitted development rules, similar in spirit but with different limits. Write one short section per nation only after checking the official source (mygov.scot, gov.wales, nidirect.gov.uk). [Do not publish this section unverified.]

### FAQ
**Can I put a log cabin at the bottom of my garden without planning permission?** Usually yes, if it is within 2 m of the boundary and no higher than 2.5 m.
**Can I use a garden office without permission?** Yes, as long as it is used as part of your home and not as a separate business open to the public.
**Can I sleep in my log cabin?** Occasional use as a guest room is a grey area; regular sleeping or renting needs permission and building regs.

# ideaswood.eu / .com / .com.au – cross-domain setup (all three owned by IdeasWood)

Decision 2026-09-29 (Justinas): all three domains stay. No 301 merge. ideaswood.com is handled in a separate session.

## Why something still has to be done
Google currently sees the same English pages on .eu and .com (same slugs, same guide), so it picks one and filters the other, and brand search splits across 4 domains. Without signals, .eu and .com compete with each other for every keyword.

## Fix: hreflang + self-canonicals (both sites must carry the same tags)
| Domain | hreflang | Market |
|---|---|---|
| ideaswood.eu | en-GB, en-IE, en (x-default) | UK, Ireland, EU (EUR, metric) |
| ideaswood.com | en-US (+ en-CA if relevant) | North America (USD, ft) |
| ideaswood.com.au | en-AU, en-NZ | Australia / NZ |

On each equivalent page, all three sites list all versions, e.g. on ideaswood.eu/the-ultimate-log-cabin-guide/:
```html
<link rel="canonical" href="https://ideaswood.eu/the-ultimate-log-cabin-guide/">
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="en-GB" href="https://ideaswood.eu/the-ultimate-log-cabin-guide/">
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="en-IE" href="https://ideaswood.eu/the-ultimate-log-cabin-guide/">
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="en-US" href="https://ideaswood.com/the-ultimate-log-cabin-guide/">
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="en-AU" href="https://ideaswood.com.au/the-ultimate-log-cabin-guide/">
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="x-default" href="https://ideaswood.eu/the-ultimate-log-cabin-guide/">
```
Rules: tags must be reciprocal (a page missing the return tag is ignored); only pair pages that really are equivalent; each page canonicals to itself, never to the other domain.
