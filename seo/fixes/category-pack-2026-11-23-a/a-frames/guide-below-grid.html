How to choose an A-frame house kit

Size and rooms. A-frame 32 and A-frame 39 have 2 rooms and stand 5.85 m high; A-frame 32 counts a 6.7 m² attic in its 32.7 m². A-frame Moose is the largest: 52 m² inside, 5 rooms, a 3.4 m² balcony and a 5.7 m² terrace, on a 5.95 x 7.1 m building with 5.67 m to the ridge. Check the full plan and the base footprint on each product page before you measure the plot.

Timber frame or log walls. A-frame 32 and 39 are timber frame A-frames in pine. A-frame 39 and A-frame Moose list 44 mm walls, and Moose has 19 mm floor and roof boards. Looking for an A-frame log cabin with solid log walls? See our garden log cabins and summer houses and chalets.

Insulation and options. What the set includes is listed on each product page. Insulation and other options are priced separately, so you choose what your use needs. For a larger home, compare our insulated wooden houses and villas and country houses.

Base, delivery and budget

An A-frame needs a firm, level base at least the size of its footprint, such as a concrete slab, paving slabs or ground screws. The kit arrives pre-cut and numbered with an assembly manual; a building of this size is a job for a builder or an experienced team. Transport is calculated per order, so tell us your address or country. The kit, transport, base, insulation and electrics are separate costs: see how much a garden cabin costs.

A-frame FAQ

A-frame 32 and A-frame 39 are timber frame buildings in pine. A-frame 39 and A-frame Moose list 44 mm walls on the product page.

Do you deliver A-frame kits across Europe?

Yes. We deliver to the UK, Ireland and the rest of Europe, and transport is calculated per order.

Can I use an A-frame as a home or holiday let?

A building used for overnight stays or as a home usually needs planning permission and building regulations approval. Read our planning permission guide and ask your local planning authority first.
