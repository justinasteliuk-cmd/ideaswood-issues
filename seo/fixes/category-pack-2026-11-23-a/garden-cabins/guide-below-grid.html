How to choose a garden log cabin

Start with the use. A cabin for summer afternoons needs less than a room you use every day. For a dedicated workspace, see our garden offices and studios. For bigger buildings with more rooms, see summer houses and chalets, and for tools and bikes, sheds and storage cabins.

Size and rooms. The smallest cabins, such as Eric 3 x 2.5 (6 m²) and Erna 2.5 x 3.4 (6.9 m²), suit a hobby room or a reading room. Around 10 to 15 m², for example Linus 4 x 3, Isley 5 x 3 or Oslo 3 x 6, takes a desk, shelving and a small sofa. From 20 m² up, such as Linus 6 x 6, Alma 7 x 8 or Padova B 6 x 6.5, there is space for a bed or a meeting table, and several models have two or three rooms.

Wall thickness. 54 of the cabins in this category have 44 mm logs. Torino 4.5 x 6 has 58 mm logs, and 68 Ottawa 6 x 4.5 and Det 6 x 6 have 68 mm logs. Thicker logs give more mass; our wall thickness guide explains the difference.

Insulation, sets and options

Most heat is lost through the floor and roof, so for a cabin used in winter plan floor and roof insulation and a heater. Insulation is an option, priced separately. Some models come as an Economic or a Premium set, and many list double glazing: the product page shows what each set contains.

Base, permission and budget

Every cabin needs a firm, level base, such as a concrete slab, paving slabs or ground screws. Read our log cabin planning permission guide before you order. The kit, transport, base, insulation and electrics are separate costs: see how much a garden cabin costs. Transport is calculated per order.
