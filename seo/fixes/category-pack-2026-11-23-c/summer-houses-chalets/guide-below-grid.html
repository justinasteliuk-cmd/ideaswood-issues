How to choose a wooden summer house

Summer house or garden cabin. A garden cabin is usually one room for daily use, such as an office or studio. A wooden summer house has several rooms and often a terrace, for stays in the warmer months; the larger, house-like layouts are the chalets.

Size and rooms. The listed footprints are 3 x 5.9 m (Pod), 4.5 x 6 m (Torino), 5.2 x 10.2 m (Typ 2), 6 x 6 m (Louise, Gustav A), 6 x 8 m (Gustav B), 6 x 8.87 m (Almeria), 6.36 x 8.86 m (Iberica T2), 6.5 x 7.7 m (Lukas), 7 x 7.19 m (Hakan B), 7 x 10.2 m (Scarlett), 8 x 9 m (Orlando) and 7.92 x 11.84 m (Iberica T3). Where the room count is listed, layouts have two to five rooms; Gustav A and Gustav B also come in WC versions. The floor plan is on each product page.

Wall thickness. One model has 28 mm walls, 11 have 44 mm, 8 have 58 mm and 11 have 68 mm. 44 mm suits spring to autumn use. 58 and 68 mm logs are the choice if you want to stay longer in the year and add insulation and heating. Our wall thickness guide compares them.

Terrace and shutters. Terraces reach 21.3 m² on the Gustav B 6 x 8 m Premium. Shutters are listed on 15 models.

Insulation, base and permission

Insulation and other options are priced separately from the kit; see insulation kits. Every summer house needs a firm, level base. A summer house used as a home or holiday let needs planning permission and building regulations approval; read our UK planning permission guide. Treat the timber soon after assembly.
