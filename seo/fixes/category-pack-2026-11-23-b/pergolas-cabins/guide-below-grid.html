How to choose a pergola cabin

Size and covered area. Linsi and Luna have a 2.8 x 3.8 m base and 9.8 m² of covered area. Haga and Kipa have a 3.8 x 3.8 m base and 13.4 m² of covered area. All four have 2.16 m side walls and a 2.63 m ridge. Deluxe runs from 9 m² (3 x 3 m) to 24 m² (4 x 6 m) of internal area at 2.5 m high.

Roof overhang. Kipa has a 0.7 m roof overhang and a 20.9 m² roof, against 0.2 m and 18.7 m² on Haga, so it shades more of the ground around it.

Glazing. Choose an ALU version of Luna or Haga for an aluminium sliding window system with 4 mm tempered glass. Deluxe has two sides of anthracite (RAL 7016) aluminium sliding windows with 4 mm safety glass and two sides of 19 mm cladding; glazed and solid sides can swap places during assembly, so you can face the glass towards the view.

Base. Fix the posts to a firm base as the manual shows. Deluxe uses Rothoblaas column supports on a concrete foundation or a wooden base, and its wood is supplied untreated. In the UK, read our planning permission guide first.

For an open pergola, see the gable-roof wooden pergolas; for a wall-mounted cover, the aluminium pergola kits. A pergola cabin also suits a BBQ grill cabin or garden furniture nearby.

Pergola cabin FAQ

What is the difference between Pergola-Luna and Pergola-Luna-ALU?

The size and timber are the same (3 x 4 m, 68 mm). The ALU version adds an aluminium sliding window system with 4 mm tempered glass. The same applies to Haga and Haga-ALU at 4 x 4 m.

Which pergola cabin is the largest?

Deluxe 4 x 6 m, with 24 m² of internal area. Among Linsi, Luna, Haga and Kipa, Haga and Kipa are largest at 13.4 m² covered.

Can I choose where the glass goes on Deluxe?

Yes. Deluxe has two glazed and two clad sides, and they can swap places during assembly to suit your view and layout.
