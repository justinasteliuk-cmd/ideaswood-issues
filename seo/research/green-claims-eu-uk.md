## 1. Directive (EU) 2024/825 (EmpCo)
- Amends the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC and the Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU. Applies to B2C practices only. [summary only] H
  - https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/825/oj/eng
- Dates: Member States adopt and publish national measures by 27 March 2026; the measures apply from 27 September 2026 with no transition period. The claim the seller was told is correct on date, but it is a national-law obligation (see 2) and the wording is "without proof" only in part (see the table). [summary only] H
  - https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/825/oj/eng ; https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/the-business-environment/empowering-consumers-for-the-green-transition/
- New blacklist items (Annex I UCPD). Numbering and exact wording NOT verified; proposal-stage numbering was 2a, 4a, 4b, 4c, 10a. [summary only] M
  - Displaying a sustainability label not based on a certification scheme or not established by public authorities.
  - Making a generic environmental claim without demonstrating recognised excellent environmental performance relevant to the claim.
  - Making an environmental claim about the entire product/business when it concerns only a certain aspect.
  - Claiming a product has a neutral, reduced or positive environmental impact (e.g. climate neutral) based on greenhouse-gas offsetting.
  - Presenting legal requirements that apply to all products in the category as a distinctive feature of the offer.
  - Future environmental performance claims (e.g. "carbon neutral by 2035") are misleading unless backed by clear, objective, public, verifiable commitments in a detailed realistic implementation plan, with regular independent expert verification (this is in the amended UCPD Art. 6, not the blacklist).
- Definition of generic claim: any environmental claim in written or oral form, including audiovisual, that is not on a sustainability label and where the specification is not given in clear and prominent terms on the same medium (same ad / web page / packaging; a link elsewhere is not enough). Specified claims are not generic. [summary only] M
  - https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/3c257883-bb2a-4dd9-a6dc-501d587bb34f_en?filename=faq-empowerting-consumers-gtd.pdf (Commission FAQ; not read)
- Example generic terms in the recitals: "environmentally friendly", "eco-friendly", "green", "nature's friend", "ecological", "climate friendly", "gentle on the environment", "biodegradable" ("energy efficient" and "biobased" were in the user's list; not confirmed in recital by search). [summary only] M
- Recognised excellent environmental performance: compliance with the EU Ecolabel, an officially recognised national ecolabel in a Member State, EN ISO 14024 type I ecolabelling, or top environmental performance under other Union law relevant to the claim. The claim must match what the performance covers (recital example: "energy efficient" can rest on top EU energy class; "biodegradable" cannot be a generic claim on that basis). [summary only] M
- Imagery: leaf/green visuals alone are not a generic claim but can imply one with text. [summary only, practitioner guide] L
- Commission FAQ is the best primary source for terms; read it directly (see section on lawyer).

## 2. Lithuania

- 20 June 2025 the Commission announced intention to withdraw; the 23 June trilogue was cancelled. https://www.lw.com/en/insights/european-commission-announces-intention-to-withdraw-eu-green-claims-directive-proposal [summary only] H

## 4. UK
- CMA Green Claims Code (Sept 2021) still applies; the DMCC Act 2024 gives the CMA direct consumer-law enforcement from 6 April 2025, fines up to the greater of £300,000 or 10% of global turnover per breach. [summary only] M
  - https://www.reedsmith.com/en/perspectives/2025/05/green-police-expanding-cmas-enforcement-powers-on-greenwashing-in-the-uk ; https://sustainablefutures.linklaters.com/post/102ifk9/green-advertising-claims-in-the-uk-cmas-new-fining-powers-under-the-digital-mar
  - CMA supply-chain green marketing guidance 22 Jan 2026 (via law-firm note): https://www.tlt.com/insights-and-events/insight/navigating-green-claims-cma-guidance-for-supply-chain-actors [summary only] L
- UK is NOT subject to EmpCo. Law is the DMCC Act regime (Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading regs replaced by DMCC Part 4 Ch.1).
- ASA/CAP Code section 11: basis of claims must be clear; absolute claims need a high level of substantiation; claims based on part of the life cycle must not mislead about total impact; unqualified "carbon neutral"/"net zero" to be avoided; ASA ruled against "sustainable" claims by fashion brands in 2025. https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/11.html ; https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/environmental-claims-general.html [summary only] M
- Specific ASA/CMA treatment of "natural", "renewable", "recyclable" for timber: not found in searches. Treat as general rule (must be true, specific, qualified, evidence held). Mark as inference.

## 5. Timber specifics
- EUDR: application date is 30 December 2026 for large and medium operators/traders, and (per secondary sources) also for micro/small operators handling timber (June 2027 relief reportedly for non-timber primary operators); EU Timber Regulation repealed from that date. Cabins fall under wood/wood articles in EUDR scope (confirm HS code of cabins/prefab buildings in Annex I). Commission simplification report May 2026; sources say no further delay expected. [summary only, compliance blogs] L-M
  - https://www.coolset.com/academy/eudr-delay-2025-explained ; https://getbindu.com/eudr/deadlines (not official; verify at https://environment.ec.europa.eu and Regulation (EU) 2025/2650 on EUR-Lex)
- EUDR is a legality/deforestation due-diligence law, not a marketing-claims law; "EUDR compliant" is a factual compliance claim and should only be used if a due-diligence statement exists.
- Species, origin (country/region of harvest and of manufacture), thickness, treatment: factual, verifiable, safe if records exist.
- FSC/PEFC: usable only with a valid chain-of-custody certificate and licence for the seller's own products and logo use under the scheme's trademark rules; a certification scheme label is the type of label EmpCo permits. Claim must say what portion is certified (whole product vs part; FSC Mix/% claims). (general knowledge, not confirmed by search) M
- "Renewable", "natural", "biodegradable": not on the Commission's confirmed generic list except "biodegradable" (recital) and these are likely treated as generic or specific depending on context. See table. Treated as inference.

## 6. USA and Australia
USA (FTC Green Guides, 16 CFR Part 260; current text is the 2012 version, review opened Dec 2022 and unfinished per sources; not binding law but guides FTC/state enforcement) [summary only] M
- Unqualified general environmental benefit claims ("eco-friendly", "green") are difficult to substantiate and should be qualified.
- "Recyclable": facilities must be available to a substantial majority (about 60%) of consumers where sold, otherwise qualify.
- "Biodegradable": unqualified claim deceptive for items going to landfill (complete decomposition within one year).
- California and other states have additional laws; check before US campaigns.
- https://policyrisk.com/wiki/ftc-green-guides ; https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/01/responsible-green-marketing

Australia (ACCC "Environmental and sustainability claims" guidance, Dec 2023; Australian Consumer Law) [summary only] M
- Avoid broad vague wording such as "eco-friendly", "sustainable", "green" without qualification and evidence.
- Eight principles: truthful, substantiated, clear and not hidden qualifiers, plain language, future claims backed by plan, etc.
- Penalties for misleading conduct up to the greater of A$50m, 3x benefit, or 30% of turnover in the period (sources vary; verify).
- https://www.accc.gov.au/system/files/Environmental%20and%20sustainability%20claims%20-%20draft%20guidance%20for%20business_web.pdf (draft; final is on accc.gov.au)

## What a timber seller may say / must not say
(EU = EmpCo as transposed; UK = CMA/ASA; status is a risk reading, not legal advice)

| Phrase | Status | Needed proof |
|---|---|---|
| "eco-friendly", "environmentally friendly", "green", "ecological", "climate friendly", "gentle on the environment", "nature's friend" | EU: prohibited as generic claim unless recognised excellent performance (EU Ecolabel etc.) or specified on same medium. UK: avoid unqualified | Ecolabel certificate for the product; otherwise use a specific claim |
| "sustainable" / "sustainably built" | Same as above (generic). UK ASA has ruled against it | Replace with the specific fact, e.g. "timber FSC-certified, licence no. X" |
| "sustainably sourced timber" | Risky: generic unless specified in same place | FSC/PEFC certificate and scope on same page |
| "FSC certified" / "PEFC certified" | Allowed | Valid CoC certificate covering these products; correct logo licence; state share if partial |
| "Made from Scots pine / Nordic spruce grown in Lithuania/Finland" | Allowed (specific, factual) | Supplier species/origin records |
| "Natural material", "natural wood" | Low risk if literally true (untreated or lightly treated wood); weakly generic in some readings; avoid implying whole cabin is natural | Describe composition; disclose impregnation, glues, insulation |
| "Renewable material" / "wood is renewable" | Low-moderate risk; acceptable as specific material fact if linked to forest management/replanting | Source forests under certified management; no whole-product implication |
| "Biodegradable" | Do not use for a cabin; EmpCo recital treats it as generic and not backable by excellent performance | Only with specific test standard and scope (not a cabin) |
| "Recyclable" | Specific claim; risky for treated/painted wood; US needs access qualification | State what and where; evidence of recycling route |
| "Carbon neutral", "climate positive", "stores CO2", "carbon negative" | EU: offsetting-based neutral/reduced-impact claims blacklisted. Do not use | Only a specific, quantified, verified LCA/EPD statement (e.g. "stores X kg CO2e in wood, EPD no. Y"), no offsets netting |
| "Wood stores carbon" (general fact about wood) | Allowed if framed as a material fact, not as the cabin being climate neutral | Cite source; avoid product-level promise without EPD |
| "Low-carbon cabin" | Risky comparative/generic | EPD/LCA with stated comparator |
| "EUDR compliant" / "deforestation-free" | Allowed only if true | Due-diligence statement, legality and geolocation evidence (from 30 Dec 2026) |
| "Fully eco" claims on whole cabin when only a part is eco (e.g. certified wood but non-certified insulation) | Prohibited (whole vs part) | Make claim specific to the part |
| "Meets legal requirements" (e.g. "CE marked", "complies with EU energy rules") as a selling point | Prohibited if legal minimum is presented as a distinctive feature | Do not market it as special |
| Own leaf badge, "eco certified" self-made logos | Prohibited (sustainability label without certification scheme/public authority) | Third-party scheme or public authority only |
| "Carbon neutral by 2030" | Prohibited without detailed public verified plan | Plan, independent expert check |
| "Energy efficient" | Allowed if specified and backed (e.g. U-value, class) | Test data; ecolabel/class if used generically |
