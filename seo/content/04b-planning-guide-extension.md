### H2: Do you need planning permission for a log cabin holiday let?

Usually yes. Permitted development for outbuildings in England only covers a building used for a purpose incidental to enjoying your house [1]. The Planning Portal says such a building must not itself be separate, self-contained living accommodation [2], and councils say living accommodation separate from the house needs planning permission [3]. Letting a cabin to paying guests is a separate use, so plan on applying for planning permission, whether the cabin is in your garden or on other land.

England has no separate planning use class for short-term lets yet. The government consulted on one (a proposed class C5), but it is not in force [4] [CHECK: confirm status on gov.uk before publishing]. A national register of short-term and holiday lets is due to be operational by March 2027 [5].

A cabin with sleeping accommodation is also not exempt from building regulations [6]. Check with your local planning authority before you order.

### H2: Holiday log cabins in an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty or National Park

Expect a full planning application, with stricter tests. Holiday cabins are not permitted development anywhere (see above), and in protected areas even garden outbuildings face tighter limits: in National Parks, the Broads, Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty (now called National Landscapes) and World Heritage Sites, buildings more than 20 m from the house may cover no more than 10 m² in total [1] [2].

When the council decides an application, national policy asks it to give special weight to conserving landscape and scenic beauty in National Parks, the Broads and National Landscapes [7]. The National Planning Policy Framework was replaced on 17 August 2026 [8], so check the current wording [CHECK: exact wording of the protected-landscapes policy in the August 2026 NPPF]. Many National Park authorities and councils also publish their own tourism and holiday-accommodation policies.

Ask your local planning authority, or the National Park authority, for pre-application advice before you buy.

### H2: Do you need planning permission for two log cabins as holiday lets in Scotland?

Yes, in almost every case. Scottish Government guidance on short-term lets says planning permission is required for all "development" [9], and council guidance treats placing a hut or similar building on land as development that needs permission [10]. Garden buildings built or converted for short-term let use do not benefit from householder permitted development rights [11].

Three other rules apply:

- **Licence.** Every short-term let in Scotland, including holiday cottages, pods and other unconventional accommodation, needs a short-term let licence, and all hosts needed one from 1 January 2025 [12].
- **Control areas.** In a short-term let control area, changing a dwelling to a short-term let counts as a material change of use [13]. The City of Edinburgh and Badenoch and Strathspey are listed as control areas [14] [CHECK: current list].
- **Building warrant.** This is a separate approval from planning [11] [15].

Contact your council's planning and building standards teams first.

### H2: Do I need planning permission for a garden log cabin in Scotland?

Often not, if it meets Scotland's own permitted development rules. Under Class 3A, no part of the building may be higher than 4 m, and any part within 1 m of your boundary may be no higher than 2.5 m [16]. Eaves must be no higher than 3 m [17]. In a conservation area or the grounds of a listed building, the footprint is limited to 4 m² [17] [CHECK: confirm conservation-area wording in Circular 1/2024]. The cabin must not be used as a separate dwelling [18].

A building warrant is a separate question. A detached single-storey building ancillary to a house is exempt up to 8 m², with exceptions such as a flue, a fixed combustion appliance or a sanitary facility [19]. Above that, check with building standards: one council checklist says a warrant is needed over 30 m², within 1 m of a boundary, or with a combustion appliance or sanitary facility [15] [CHECK: rules for 8 to 30 m²].

The same rules apply in Aberdeenshire; check with your council.

### H2: Can you live in a log cabin as a permanent residence?

Not under permitted development. In England, an outbuilding built under permitted development must not be separate, self-contained living accommodation [2], and a cabin used as a separate home needs planning permission [3]. The same applies in Scotland, where permitted development does not cover a building used as a separate dwelling [18].

A home also falls outside the building regulations exemption for small detached buildings, which only covers buildings with no sleeping accommodation [6]. Expect to need full building regulations approval (a building warrant in Scotland) as well as planning permission.

Some holiday lodges are treated in law as caravans rather than buildings, which changes the rules that apply. Councils decide this case by case, using the statutory definition of a caravan [20]. Ask your local planning authority before you plan to live in a cabin.

### H2: Do you need planning permission for a log cabin on agricultural land?

Yes, for living or holiday use. Agricultural permitted development rights in England (Part 6 of the General Permitted Development Order) cover buildings reasonably necessary for agriculture on the farm [21], not cabins for guests or residents [CHECK: confirm the residential exclusion in Part 6 Class A conditions]. Councils treat siting a holiday lodge or similar unit as development to be assessed, starting with whether the unit is a caravan or a building [20]. A planning appeal found that stationing a caravan on farmland for living in could be a material change of use to mixed agricultural and residential use [22].

Holiday use usually comes with planning conditions, such as holiday-only occupancy [20].

In Scotland, placing a hut or temporary building on land for any period counts as development needing permission [10]. Ask your local planning authority for pre-application advice.

### H2: Log cabin building regulations in the UK

Building regulations are separate from planning permission. In England, a detached single-storey building with a floor area up to 30 m² and no sleeping accommodation can be exempt [6]; the 15 m² and 30 m² conditions are in the table above. A cabin for sleeping, a holiday let or a home is not covered by that exemption [6].

Wales uses the same size bands for outbuildings [23]. In Northern Ireland, a detached single-storey building up to 30 m² with no sleeping accommodation is exempt if it is substantially non-combustible or at least 1 m from the house, road and boundary [24]. In Scotland the approval is a building warrant, with its own exemptions (see the Scotland section) [19].

Electrical work in an outbuilding must also meet the regulations (see above). Check with your local building control or building standards team.

### H2: Wales and Northern Ireland in brief

**Wales.** Outbuilding rules are close to England's, but not identical: the 4 m limit covers dual-pitched or hipped roofs [23] [CHECK: wording on gov.wales]. Wales has separate use classes for main homes (C3), second homes (C5) and short-term lets (C6). Moving between them is permitted development unless an Article 4 Direction removes it [25], as in Gwynedd and Eryri National Park [26] [CHECK: current status of the Gwynedd direction after the November 2025 court decision].

**Northern Ireland.** Permitted development is set by the Planning (General Permitted Development) Order (Northern Ireland) 2015, and rights can be restricted in designated areas [27]. Your council's planning office confirms what applies [27].

## FAQ (replace the existing "Planning permission questions" block, keeping its 5 current answers below these, or merge to 8-10 Qs total; use FAQ schema only for Qs visible on the page)

**Do I need planning permission for a log cabin holiday let?**
Usually yes. Permitted development only covers a garden building used for a purpose incidental to your house, not separate living accommodation [1] [2]. Letting it to guests is a different use, so apply to your local planning authority first. Building regulations also apply if it has sleeping accommodation [6].

**Do I need planning permission for holiday log cabins in an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty?**
Yes, expect a full application. National policy asks councils to give special weight to landscape and scenic beauty in National Landscapes (AONBs), National Parks and the Broads [7]. Ask the council or National Park authority for pre-application advice.

**Do you need planning permission to build two log cabins as holiday lets in Scotland?**
Yes. Planning permission is required for all development [9], and each short-term let also needs a short-term let licence [12]. A building warrant is a separate approval [15]. Talk to your council's planning and building standards teams.

**Do I need planning permission for a log cabin in my garden?**
Often not in England, if it meets the height, position, size and use rules above [2]. In Scotland the limits differ: 4 m overall, and 2.5 m within 1 m of a boundary [16]. Check with your local planning authority if any rule does not fit.

**Can I live in a log cabin permanently?**
Not under permitted development. A cabin used as a separate home needs planning permission [2] [3], and it is not exempt from building regulations because it contains sleeping accommodation [6]. Ask your local planning authority before you plan to live in one.
