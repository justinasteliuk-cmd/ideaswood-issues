### H2: Do you need planning permission for a log cabin holiday let?

Usually yes. Permitted development for outbuildings in England only covers a building required for a purpose incidental to the enjoyment of your house [1] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/596/schedule/2/part/1/crossheading/class-e-buildings-etc-incidental-to-the-enjoyment-of-a-dwellinghouse)*. The Planning Portal says these rules do not cover any other change of use of the land or outbuilding, or use as separate self-contained living accommodation [2] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.planningportal.co.uk/permission/common-projects/outbuildings/planning-permission)*. Government technical guidance adds that an incidental purpose does not cover separate self-contained accommodation or primary living accommodation such as a bedroom, bathroom or kitchen [3] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5d77afc8e5274a27cdb2c9e9/190910_Tech_Guide_for_publishing.pdf, Class E section)*. Letting a cabin to paying guests is not a use incidental to your own home, so plan on applying for planning permission, whether the cabin is in your garden or on other land. GOV.UK guidance for holiday-let owners says your local planning authority decides whether you need planning permission, and tells you to contact your council to confirm [5] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/letting-out-a-self-catering-holiday-home-in-england-rules-and-regulations)*.

England has no planning use class for short-term lets. The government consulted in 2023 on a new class, "C5 Short Term Let" [4] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/introduction-of-a-use-class-for-short-term-lets-and-associated-permitted-development-rights)*; GOV.UK still shows the outcome as "We are analysing your feedback", and the Use Classes Order on legislation.gov.uk contains classes C5 and C6 for Wales only [4] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1987/764/schedule)*. A mandatory national registration scheme for short-term lets in England is not yet in force; GOV.UK says it is expected to begin in March 2027 [5] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/letting-out-a-self-catering-holiday-home-in-england-rules-and-regulations, page reviewed 2 October 2026)*.

A cabin with sleeping accommodation is also not exempt from building regulations [6] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2010/2214/schedule/2/part/6)*. Check with your local planning authority before you order.

### H2: Holiday log cabins in an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty or National Park

Expect a full planning application, with stricter tests. Holiday cabins are not permitted development anywhere (see above), and in protected areas even garden outbuildings face tighter limits: in National Parks, the Broads, Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty (now called National Landscapes) and World Heritage Sites, buildings more than 20 m from the house may cover no more than 10 m² in total [1] [2] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/596/schedule/2/part/1/crossheading/class-e-buildings-etc-incidental-to-the-enjoyment-of-a-dwellinghouse, para E.2; https://www.planningportal.co.uk/permission/common-projects/outbuildings/planning-permission)*.

The National Planning Policy Framework published on 17 August 2026 replaces the December 2024 version [8] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/national-planning-policy-framework and https://www.gov.uk/guidance/national-planning-policy-framework-chapter-1-introduction, para 1)*. Its policy N4 on Protected Landscapes (National Parks, the Broads and National Landscapes) says development there should be limited in scale and extent, and sensitively located and designed, and that substantial weight should be placed on conserving and enhancing the natural beauty of these areas. Major development should be refused other than in exceptional circumstances, and development in the setting of a Protected Landscape should also be sensitively located and designed [7] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/national-planning-policy-framework-chapter-19-conserving-and-enhancing-the-natural-environment-n1-6, policy N4; definitions in https://www.gov.uk/guidance/national-planning-policy-framework-annex-b-glossary)*. Many National Park authorities and councils also publish their own tourism and holiday-accommodation policies.

Ask your local planning authority, or the National Park authority, for pre-application advice before you buy.

### H2: Do you need planning permission for two log cabins as holiday lets in Scotland?

Expect to need it. Scottish Government guidance on short-term lets says planning permission is required for all "development" [9] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.gov.scot/publications/short-term-lets-scotland-planning-guidance-hosts-operators-2/pages/2/, para 2.1)*, and the planning Act defines development as building and other operations on land or any material change in the use of buildings or land [10] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1997/8/section/26)*. Householder permitted development (Class 3A) only covers a building for a purpose incidental to enjoying your house, and not one that is a dwelling [16] [18] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/223/schedule/1, Class 3A; https://www.gov.scot/publications/circular-1-2024-householder-permitted-development-rights/pages/4/, para 4.60)*.

Three other rules apply:

- **Licence.** The short-term let licensing scheme is mandatory for all short-term let accommodation in Scotland, including holiday cottages and unconventional accommodation such as pods and yurts, however long you let for. Since the law changed on 1 October 2022, new hosts must get a licence from the council before accepting bookings or receiving guests [12] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.gov.scot/publications/short-term-lets/pages/licensing-scheme/)*.
- **Control areas.** In a short-term let control area, changing a house or flat to a short-term let is treated as a material change of use (with some exceptions) and so always needs planning permission [13] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.gov.scot/publications/short-term-lets-planning/pages/3/, para 3.1)*. The Scottish Government names Edinburgh (designated 5 September 2022) and the Badenoch and Strathspey ward of Highland (4 March 2024) as control areas [12] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.gov.scot/publications/short-term-lets/pages/licensing-scheme/)*; ask your council whether others now apply.
- **Building warrant.** This is a separate approval from planning, with its own exemptions (see the next section) [15] [19].

Contact your council's planning and building standards teams first.

### H2: Do I need planning permission for a garden log cabin in Scotland?

Often not, if it meets Scotland's own permitted development rules (Class 3A). No part of the building may be higher than 4 m, any part within 1 m of your boundary may be no higher than 2.5 m, and the eaves may be no higher than 3 m. It must not be forward of a wall forming the principal or side elevation where that elevation fronts a road, and development may not cover more than half of the front or rear garden. In a conservation area the footprint is limited to 8 m², and in the grounds of a listed building to 4 m². The building must not be a dwelling [16] [18] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/223/schedule/1, Class 3A; https://www.gov.scot/publications/circular-1-2024-householder-permitted-development-rights/pages/4/, paras 4.60 to 4.66)*.

A building warrant is a separate question. A detached single-storey building ancillary to a house is exempt up to 8 m², unless it is within 1 m of the house (and not at least 1 m from every boundary), contains sleeping accommodation, or has a flue, a fixed combustion appliance or a sanitary facility [19] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.gov.scot/publications/building-standards-technical-handbook-2022-domestic/0-general/0-3-exempted-buildings-services-fittings-equipment/, type 17; same wording in the April 2026 handbook, Table 0.1)*. From 8 m² to 30 m², such a building does not need a warrant but must still meet the building standards, unless it is within 1 m of the house (and not at least 1 m from every boundary) or has a fixed combustion appliance or a sanitary facility [15] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.gov.scot/publications/building-standards-technical-handbook-domestic-april-2026-pdf/, Table 0.3, Schedule 3 type 4)*. Above 30 m², or outside these conditions, check with building standards.

The same rules apply in Aberdeenshire; check with your council.

### H2: Can you live in a log cabin as a permanent residence?

Not under permitted development. In England, the outbuilding rules do not cover use as separate self-contained living accommodation [2] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.planningportal.co.uk/permission/common-projects/outbuildings/planning-permission)*, and an incidental purpose does not include primary living accommodation such as a bedroom, bathroom or kitchen [3] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5d77afc8e5274a27cdb2c9e9/190910_Tech_Guide_for_publishing.pdf)*. In Scotland, Class 3A permitted development does not cover a building that is a dwelling [16] [18] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/223/schedule/1; https://www.gov.scot/publications/circular-1-2024-householder-permitted-development-rights/pages/4/)*.

A home also falls outside the building regulations exemption for small detached buildings, which only covers buildings with no sleeping accommodation [6] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2010/2214/schedule/2/part/6)*. Expect to need full building regulations approval (a building warrant in Scotland) as well as planning permission.

Some holiday lodges fall within the legal definition of a caravan rather than a building: a structure designed or adapted for human habitation that can be moved from one place to another. A twin-unit structure assembled on site still counts if, when assembled, it is no more than 20 m long, 6.8 m wide and 3.05 m high inside [20] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Eliz2/8-9/62/section/29; https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1968/52/section/13)*. That changes which rules apply. Ask your local planning authority before you plan to live in a cabin.

### H2: Do you need planning permission for a log cabin on agricultural land?

Yes, for living or holiday use. Agricultural permitted development in England (Part 6, Class A of the General Permitted Development Order) covers buildings reasonably necessary for agriculture on a unit of 5 hectares or more. It does not cover the erection of a dwelling, or a building not designed for agricultural purposes [21] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/596/schedule/2/part/6/crossheading/class-a-agricultural-development-on-units-of-5-hectares-or-more, paras A and A.1(c), A.1(d))*. If a lodge meets the legal definition of a caravan, different rules apply (see above) [20].

In Scotland, using land for agriculture is not development, but building operations and material changes of use are [10] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1997/8/section/26)*. Ask your local planning authority for pre-application advice.

### H2: Log cabin building regulations in the UK

Building regulations are separate from planning permission. In England, a detached single-storey building with a floor area up to 15 m² and no sleeping accommodation is exempt; up to 30 m² it is exempt if it has no sleeping accommodation and is at least 1 m from the boundary or built substantially of non-combustible material [6] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2010/2214/schedule/2/part/6; https://www.planningportal.co.uk/permission/common-projects/outbuildings/is-building-regulations-approval-needed-for-an-outbuilding)*. A cabin for sleeping, a holiday let or a home is not covered by that exemption [6].

Wales: the same Schedule 2 Class VI exemption is shown on legislation.gov.uk as extending to England and Wales [6] [23] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2010/2214/schedule/2/part/6)*. In Northern Ireland, a detached single-storey building up to 30 m² with no sleeping accommodation is exempt if it is built substantially of non-combustible material or is at no point less than 1 m from the nearest dwelling, road or boundary; up to 15 m² it is exempt if it has no sleeping accommodation and is at least 1 m from a dwelling [24] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nisr/2012/192/schedule/2, Class 7)*. In Scotland the approval is a building warrant, with its own exemptions (see the Scotland section) [15] [19].

Electrical work in an outbuilding must also meet the building regulations (see above). Check with your local building control or building standards team.

### H2: Wales and Northern Ireland in brief

**Wales.** Outbuilding rules are close to England's, but not identical. In Wales the 4 m height limit applies to a building with a roof with more than one pitch (3 m otherwise), the 2.5 m limit applies within 2 m of the boundary, eaves are limited to 2.5 m, and any part within 2 m of the house may be no higher than 1.5 m [23] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1995/418/schedule/2/part/1, Class E, Wales text)*. Since 20 October 2022, Wales has separate use classes for main homes (C3), homes not used as a sole or main residence (C5) and commercial short-term lets (C6) [25] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2022/994/made)*. Changes between these classes are permitted development, and the same 2022 order sets the procedure councils use for Article 4 directions that withdraw permitted development rights [25] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2022/997/made)*. Ask your council whether a direction applies in your area.

**Northern Ireland.** Permitted development is set by the Planning (General Permitted Development) Order (Northern Ireland) 2015. A garden building must be for a purpose incidental to the house, no higher than 4 m, with eaves no higher than 2.5 m within 2 m of the boundary, not forward of a wall facing a road, and not for use as a dwelling; in a World Heritage Site, National Park or AONB, buildings more than 20 m from the house may cover no more than 10 m² [27] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nisr/2015/70/schedule/part/1, Class D)*. Your council's planning office confirms what applies.

## FAQ (replace the existing "Planning permission questions" block, keeping its 5 current answers below these, or merge to 8-10 Qs total; use FAQ schema only for Qs visible on the page)

**Do I need planning permission for a log cabin holiday let?**
Usually yes. Permitted development only covers a garden building used for a purpose incidental to your house, not separate living accommodation [1] [2] *(Verified 2026-11-21: URLs as in the holiday-let section)*. Letting it to guests is a different use, so apply to your local planning authority first [5]. Building regulations also apply if it has sleeping accommodation [6].

**Do I need planning permission for holiday log cabins in an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty?**
Yes, expect a full application. National policy says development in National Landscapes (the new name for AONBs), National Parks and the Broads should be limited in scale and extent, and gives substantial weight to conserving their natural beauty [7] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/national-planning-policy-framework-chapter-19-conserving-and-enhancing-the-natural-environment-n1-6)*. Ask the council or National Park authority for pre-application advice.

**Do you need planning permission to build two log cabins as holiday lets in Scotland?**
Expect to. Planning permission is required for all development [9], and every short-term let needs a short-term let licence from the council before it takes bookings [12] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.gov.scot/publications/short-term-lets-scotland-planning-guidance-hosts-operators-2/pages/2/; https://www.gov.scot/publications/short-term-lets/pages/licensing-scheme/)*. A building warrant is a separate approval [15] [19]. Talk to your council's planning and building standards teams.

**Do I need planning permission for a log cabin in my garden?**
Often not in England, if it meets the height, position, size and use rules above [2]. In Scotland the limits differ: 4 m overall, 2.5 m within 1 m of a boundary, and 8 m² in a conservation area [16] [18] *(Verified 2026-11-21: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/223/schedule/1)*. Check with your local planning authority if any rule does not fit.

**Can I live in a log cabin permanently?**
Not under permitted development. A cabin used as a separate home needs planning permission [2] [3], and it is not exempt from building regulations because it contains sleeping accommodation [6] *(Verified 2026-11-21: URLs as in the permanent-residence section)*. Ask your local planning authority before you plan to live in one.
