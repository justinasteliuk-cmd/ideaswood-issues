### Summer house, garden cabin or chalet: what is the difference?
We use "summer house" for a garden building made mainly for relaxing and leisure use, and "chalet" for a larger, more house-like layout [CHECK: Justinas/Astra to confirm how the live category separates summer houses, chalets and garden cabins, and which models sit in each]. ### Choosing the wall thickness
Our garden cabins come in 44, 58 and 68 mm walls, and most are 44 mm. Sheds come in 28 and 44 mm. [CHECK: wall thickness options for each summer-house model.] As a guide: 44 mm suits summer use and, with floor and roof insulation and a heater, a garden office or studio. 58 mm gives more mass for a guest or hobby room used often. 68 mm is the thickest and is the choice for living space. Our [link: wall thickness guide] explains each one.

### Insulation and windows
Most heat is lost through the floor and roof, so insulating them often matters more than adding wall thickness. Insulation and other options are priced separately from the kit. [CHECK: which insulation options each summer-house model offers.] See our [link: insulation kits guide].

### Before you order
- **Size and layout:** draw it in the 3D IdeasPlanner first.
- **Permission:** many garden buildings fit under permitted development in England, but height, boundary distance and use all matter. A summer house used as a home or holiday let needs planning permission and building regulations approval. Read our [link: planning permission guide].
- **Total budget:** the kit, transport, base, electrics and insulation are separate costs. See [link: how much does a garden cabin cost].
- **Transport:** calculated individually for each order.
