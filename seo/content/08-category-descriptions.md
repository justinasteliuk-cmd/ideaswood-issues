**Below grid**
## How to choose a garden cabin
Start with how you will use it. A cabin for a few summer afternoons needs less than a room you will work in all winter.

| Use | Wall thickness | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Storage, hobby room, summer use | 28 mm | Light and economical. Not for winter heating. |
| Home office, studio, gym | 44 mm | The usual choice for year-round use with floor and roof insulation and a heater. |
| Guest room, holiday let, living space | 58–68 mm | Thicker logs hold heat better and carry larger spans. |

**Size.** Measure the space, then leave room around the cabin for maintenance. A 3x3 m cabin suits a desk and a shelf. For a meeting table or a bed, look at {{larger_size_example}}.

**What is in the kit.** Pre-cut logs, floor, roof structure, doors, windows and an assembly manual. {{what_is_included_from_product_page}}

**Foundation and assembly.** The cabin needs a level base such as a concrete slab, paving or ground screws. Two people can assemble most garden cabins from the manual.

**Planning permission.** In England many garden buildings fall under permitted development if they respect height and distance-from-boundary limits. See our [link: UK planning permission guide].

**Delivery.** We ship the kit on pallets to the UK, Ireland and the rest of Europe. See [link: delivery to the UK].

**Related:** [link: Summer houses and chalets], [link: Sheds and storage cabins], [link: How much does a garden cabin cost].

## Garden cabin questions
**What is the best wall thickness for a garden office?** 44 mm is the usual choice when you add floor and roof insulation and a heater. Use 58 or 68 mm if you want better heat retention in cold months.
**Can I use a garden cabin in winter?** Yes, with insulation and heating. The cabin is not heated by the logs alone.
**How long does assembly take?** It depends on size. A small cabin is a one to two day job for two people; larger ones take longer.
