## H1: Electric or Wood-Burning Outdoor Sauna: Which to Choose?

*Last updated: 24 November 2026*

**Short answer:** choose a wood-burning stove if you want the traditional fire and wood smell, have room for a flue and logs, or have no mains power at the sauna. Choose an electric heater if you want push-button use, no smoke or ash, and can bring a supply in with a qualified electrician. Both heat sauna stones the same way.

The rest of this guide goes through the questions that decide it: power, flue, daily use, where the sauna stands, local rules and which of our outdoor saunas take which heater.

## What is the real difference between the two?

Both heaters do the same job: they heat a mass of sauna stones, and the stones heat the room. You pour water on the stones for steam (löyly) in both cases. The difference is the energy source and everything that comes with it.

- **Wood-burning stove:** a firebox under the stones, a flue pipe out through the roof or wall, and logs to feed it. You light it, tend it and let it burn down.
- **Electric heater:** heating elements under the stones, fed by a fixed electrical supply, with controls to switch it on and set the temperature. There is no fire and no flue.

## When is a wood-burning sauna the better choice?

A wood-burning outdoor sauna suits you if:

- **The sauna stands away from the house.** Running a cable down a long garden or across a field plot can be the larger job. A stove works without mains power.
- **You want the ritual.** Lighting the fire, the sound of the logs and the smell of wood smoke are why many people want a wood barrel sauna in the first place.
- **You have space for logs.** You need a dry, covered place to store firewood near the sauna, and room to carry it in.
- **There is a safe flue route.** The flue needs the distances to walls, roof timber, trees and neighbours that the stove manual gives. Plan the position of the sauna around it, not the other way round.

On our Sauna S, M, L and XL cabins, a safety fence for wood-burning stoves and a 24 litre water tank for wood-burning stoves are listed as options. The stove itself is not part of the set.

## When is an electric outdoor sauna the better choice?

An electric outdoor sauna suits you if:

- **The sauna is near the house** and a supply can be brought in without major work.
- **You want quick, simple use.** No fire to tend and no ash to clear. You switch it on and set the temperature with the heater's controls.
- **Smoke would be a problem.** A small urban garden, close neighbours or local smoke rules can make a wood stove impractical.
- **You do not want to store logs.**

An electric heater must be connected by a qualified electrician, to a supply sized for the heater you choose. We do not give power figures here: they depend on the heater and the sauna room. Tell us the model and we will tell you what the heater needs. On Sauna S to XL a safety fence for electric heaters is listed as an option, and the heater and cables are not part of the set.

## What do both heater types need?

Whatever you choose, plan for:

- **A firm, level, dry base** under the whole sauna.
- **Ventilation.** Keep the sauna's vents clear. Fresh air in and stale air out matter with both heaters.
- **Clearances.** Follow the heater or stove manual for distances to walls and benches, and use the guard or fence it calls for.
- **Local rules.** Check with your local authority and your installer about electrics, stoves, flues and distance to boundaries before you order.
- **Safe use.** Keep towels and other flammable items away from the heater, never leave a lit stove unattended, and do not use the sauna after alcohol.

## FAQ

### Is an electric or wood-burning outdoor sauna better?
Neither is better in every garden. A wood stove gives the traditional fire and needs no mains power, but needs a flue and logs. An electric heater is simpler to use day to day, but needs a supply installed by a qualified electrician.

### Can I have an electric barrel sauna?
Yes. The Sauna Barrel 5.9 m is offered with a wood-fired or an electric heater. For the 1.7 m barrel, ask us which heater we can supply.

### Is the stove or heater included in the sauna?
On Sauna S, M, L and XL the stove and its cables are not included in the set. The barrels and the pod come with heat-resistant plates for the heater. Tell us the model and we confirm the heater for your order.

### Does a wood-burning sauna need a chimney?
It needs a flue. Its route and the clearances to walls, roof and trees come from the stove manual, and you should check local smoke and flue rules before you order.

### Can I switch from wood to electric later?
That depends on the sauna and the heater. Ask us before you order, and ask an electrician to check the supply.
