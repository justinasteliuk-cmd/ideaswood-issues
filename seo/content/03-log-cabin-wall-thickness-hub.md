### H1: Log Cabin Wall Thickness: Which One Do You Need?
Wall thickness decides how warm, strong and quiet your cabin is – and how much it costs. The short answer:

| Wall | Best for | Year-round use | Typical size |
|---|---|---|---|
| **28mm** | Storage, summer house, kids' play room | No – seasonal | up to ~12 m² |
| **44mm** | Garden office, studio, gym, hobby room | Yes, with floor & roof insulation + heating | 10–30 m² |
| **70mm** | Guest house, holiday let, living space | Yes – the best choice for daily use | 20 m²+ |

#### 28mm – light and affordable
Enough for tools, bikes and summer use. Keeps rain out and stays stable, but gets cold in winter. Choose it when you need space, not comfort.

#### 44mm – the garden office standard
The most popular choice in the UK and Europe. Solid enough for a heated office or gym all year. Add floor and roof insulation and a small heater and it is comfortable in winter.

#### 70mm – for living in
Thick logs hold heat, block noise and carry bigger spans and heavier roofs. The right choice for guest houses, holiday rentals and residential cabins.

#### What else matters (as much as thickness)
- **Timber quality:** slow-grown Nordic spruce has tighter rings – less twisting and cracking.
- **Log profile:** double tongue-and-groove joints seal better than single.
- **Windows and doors:** double glazing in all our 44mm+ cabins. [verify]
- **Insulation:** floor and roof lose more heat than walls. See our insulation kit guide.
- **Foundation:** level base, ventilation under the floor.

#### FAQ
**Is 44mm thick enough to use all year?** Yes, for an office or studio, if you insulate the floor and roof and add heating.
**Do I need 70mm to live in a log cabin?** For daily living, yes. Most residential log homes use 70mm+ or insulated double walls.
**Does a thicker wall need a bigger foundation?** The base must carry more weight; ask us for loads per model.
